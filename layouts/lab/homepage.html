Galactic center · Stellar dynamics

Were S301 and the runaway star once a pair?

One star, S5-HVS1, is speeding away from the Galactic center. Another, S301, orbits close to its black hole. We found that S301’s orbit and estimated mass fit what we would expect if Sagittarius A* broke up their binary, capturing one star and ejecting the other. Their shared origin remains unproven; comparing their chemical fingerprints could test it.

Caputo & Tomaselli · Read the paper ↗ Science: how S301 could reveal the black hole’s spin ↗

Supernovae · Particle physics

How dark photons could prevent a stellar explosion

When a massive star’s core collapses, neutrinos deposit heat just behind the stalled shock, helping it move outward again. We found that dark photons could cool that same layer enough to prevent the explosion. The effect is strongest for dark-photon masses of 0.1–0.4 MeV, where successful explosions put a new limit on their mixing with ordinary photons.

Caputo, Janka, Raffelt & Yun · Physical Review Letters ↗

Galactic dynamics

What lies inside S2’s orbit?

The star S2 orbits the Milky Way’s central black hole. We used GRAVITY’s measurement of the orbit’s precession to constrain how much unseen matter, including a possible cloud of ultralight bosons, can fit inside it.

Tomaselli & Caputo · Read paper ↗

Computational cosmology

1,024 Milky Way analogues

Why do dark-matter density profiles differ between galaxies? In this set of DREAMS simulations, differences from one halo to the next outweighed the effects of varying the strength of feedback from stars and black holes.

Garcia et al. · Read paper ↗

Radio & plasma searches

Listening to the ionosphere

If wave-like dark matter converts into radio waves in Earth’s ionosphere, a dipole antenna on the ground could pick up the signal. We calculated the emission and identified the particle masses that such an antenna could test.

Beadle, Caputo & Ellis · Read paper ↗
More papers and experiments

Dwarf galaxies

How does a dwarf galaxy heat up?

By including stellar self-gravity and estimating tidal stripping by the Milky Way, we tested which ultralight dark-matter masses remain in tension with observations of five dwarf galaxies.

Gravitational waves

Black-hole spins as particle probes

An ultralight boson can extract spin from a black hole through superradiance. We used the rapidly rotating black holes in GW231123 to constrain axions under different assumptions about how the binary formed.

Neutron stars

Axions in a pulsar’s plasma

We developed particle-in-cell simulations to follow how an axion field changes particle acceleration and electric-field screening above a pulsar’s magnetic poles.

Detector proposal

MADGRID

This proposed haloscope places a few wire grids in front of a mirror. Adjusting their spacing tunes the response to millielectronvolt axions or dark photons; external optics collect the radiation emitted from the face of the stack.

AstroDarkLS

Astro Dark Large & Small

How would a new light particle change the light we see from an exploding star, or the pattern of absorption in a distant quasar’s spectrum? AstroDarkLS brings these questions together by studying supernova light curves, the Lyman-α forest, and maps of the combined line emission from distant galaxies.
